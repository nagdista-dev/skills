## Second example: one word, two meanings (`Saw` — one card per meaning)

`Saw` is the classic dual-meaning trap: the past of `see`, and the tool that cuts wood. Under the one-card-per-meaning rule these are **two complete cards**, never one card that carries a `second_meaning` line.

- The part of speech is what differs, so the titles use the role tags: `Saw (V)` and `Saw (N)`.
- Each card owns its own note, IPA, example, pronunciation audio, example audio, hooks and gang base.
- The two cards point at each other from `word_family`, so the graph shows them as siblings.
- Neither card announces that the word has two meanings: the sibling card *is* the second meaning.

Inputs: `references/examples/saw-verb.json` and `references/examples/saw-noun.json`.

## Third example: derivational form handling (`Abandonment` & `Abandon`)

Under the updated derivational rule, non-root words (derivatives such as nouns, adjectives, or adverbs) are NEVER collapsed into the root note to suppress the derivative's card.
When the user requests a derivative like `Abandonment`:
1. **Root verification & creation:** The agent checks if the root word `Abandon` exists in the vault. If no root note exists, the agent creates the full vocabulary card for `Abandon` first.
2. **Derivative card creation:** The agent creates the full, separate vocabulary card for `Abandonment` (`Vocabulary/A/Abandonment.md`, `Abandonment_pronunciation.mp3`, and `abandonment_example.mp3`).
3. **Dual independent cards:** Both `Abandon` and `Abandonment` exist as separate, complete cards in the vault. Each card cross-references the other under its `word_family` list.

*(Note: The low-level CLI flag `build_word.py --add-family-member` remains available for programmatic vault utilities, but the skill workflow creates dual independent cards.)*
